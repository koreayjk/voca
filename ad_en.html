How to Study
📖 Learn the words first, read the next day
The most effective way to use IM VOCA is previewing.
Memorize the difficult words from tomorrow's page today,
and the next day that page reads effortlessly.
1
Today — decide tomorrow's reading
e.g. 30 min of daily reading ≈ 10 pages. Pick the page you'll read tomorrow.
2
Take a photo of that page
AI pulls only the hard words from that page, sorted by difficulty, into a word list.
3
Memorize only the words you don't know
Skip the words you know. Just a small set to learn with cards and quizzes — no overload.
4
The next day — read that page
You already know the words, so it flows. Meeting them again in the book locks them in.
💡 Why it works: Ordinary word apps rarely let you meet a word again, so it fades fast. But this method has you meet the word for real in the book the very next day. Once while previewing, again while reading, and again through Ebbinghaus review — three encounters turn it into long-term memory.
Finding Your Right Book
Is your book too hard — or too easy?
A book reads best — and teaches most — when it's at just the right level. Here's a simple way to check with IM VOCA.
📷 How to test
Scan about 5 pages of the book you're considering, then count how many of the 20 words on a page you don't know.
🎯
5–10 unknown words — just right
The sweet spot: enjoyable to read and rewarding to learn. Dive right in!
🌊
Fewer than 5 — smooth reading
Great for relaxed reading rather than study. Enjoy the story!
⛰️
15 or more — a bit challenging
Start with something easier first. Once you build confidence, come back — it'll read much more smoothly.

① CEFR International Language Standard
🎓
What is CEFR?
Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) is a globally recognized standard for measuring language proficiency.
Major tests like IELTS, TOEFL, Cambridge and educational institutions worldwide use this standard.
A1 A2
Beginner (A1-A2) — Basic words, simple daily conversation
B1 B2
Intermediate (B1-B2) — Travel and work communication
C1 C2
Advanced (C1-C2) — Reading books and papers, fluent expression
📱 How IM VOCA uses CEFR
When extracting words, AI automatically classifies by CEFR. C1~C2 hard words first, then B2 and B1.
Each word card shows CEFR C1 CEFR B2 level badges so you can see difficulty at a glance.
② Comprehensible Input
📖
Comprehensible Input Theory
Proposed by linguist Dr. Stephen Krashen
Language is acquired naturally when understood in context, not when forced.
Words learned in context are remembered far longer than words memorized alone.
Words from your own book are even more effective — you already know the context.
📱 How IM VOCA applies this
Words are saved with both base meaning + contextual meaning from your book.
Both meanings appear on the flashcard back, helping you remember words in context.

④ Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve
📊 Scientifically Proven Review Schedule
19th century German psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus studied how much people forget over time as memory fades over time.
He found that after learning, people forget 42% in 20 minutes, 67% in one day, and 79% in a month.
But reviewing just before forgetting strengthens the memory and extends the time until the next forgetting.
